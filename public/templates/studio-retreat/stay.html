The rooms.

Three rooms, each with a window worth sitting at. Let by the night or the week, described here in words rather than star ratings.

The Lantern Room

Under the eaves, looking straight down the water.

The highest room in the house and the first to get the light. The ceiling slopes, the bed sits under the window, and on a clear night the lighthouse turns across the far wall every few seconds, which is where the name comes from.

It suits one person who wants to write, or two who do not mind a staircase. The bathroom is across the landing and is yours alone.

  • One large bed
  • A desk in the window
  • Private bathroom across the landing
  • Blankets, and plenty of them
  • Books left by other people
  • No television

The Garden Room

Garden doors, and a bath deep enough to read in.

On the ground floor, at the quiet end of the house. Two glass doors open onto the garden, so breakfast can be carried out in a dressing gown when the weather is kind. The bath is the old kind, long and deep, in the room itself rather than behind a door.

The right room for anyone who would rather not climb stairs, and for anyone travelling with a dog, who will consider the garden theirs by the second morning.

  • One large bed
  • Doors to the garden
  • A deep bath in the room
  • A separate shower and basin
  • Step-free from the front door
  • A dog bed on request

The Boat Room

Two beds, panelled like a cabin, nearest the shore path.

Tongue-and-groove walls painted the colour of the estuary, two single beds a stride apart, and a round window that a previous owner brought back from a boatyard. The room children ask for by name, and the one two old friends tend to choose.

The shore path starts at the gate outside the window, which makes this the room for early swimmers and late walkers.

  • Two single beds
  • A round window to the shore
  • Shower room next door
  • Wellington boots in several sizes
  • A sea chest for wet things
  • A cot on request

Practical details

Ask anything else

The things people write to ask.

Arriving and leaving+

Rooms are ready from four in the afternoon and we ask for them back by eleven, though nobody has ever been hurried through breakfast. If we are out on the water when you arrive, the key is in the lantern by the door.

Breakfast and dinner+

Breakfast is laid out from eight and left until the last person is up. Dinner is at the long table, one sitting, and is cooked if you ask by lunchtime. Tell us what you cannot eat when you write.

Getting here+

The nearest station is a short taxi ride away and we can arrange the car. If you are driving, we will send directions for the last mile, which no map has yet got right.

Children and dogs+

Both are welcome and both tend to end up in the sea. The Boat Room takes a cot, the Garden Room takes a dog, and there are towels by the back door for either.

The weather+

It changes, often within the hour. Bring a coat you do not mind getting wet and shoes you do not mind getting sandy. There are spare boots in the porch and a drying room off the kitchen.

Booking and paying+

Write to us with your dates and who is coming, and we will reply by email with what is free and what it costs. There is no booking engine, no deposit taken online, and no rate printed on this website.

Write to us

Tell us when, and who is coming.

We answer by email, usually the same day, and we will say plainly if a week is already spoken for.
