Anatomy

The six sections of a lead generation page

Order matters less than presence: visitors scan, then read. Each block answers a question they have not asked yet.

1. A promise in the headline

Say what the visitor gets, in their words. “Get the checklist” beats “Download our PDF”. Keep the subtitle for the how and the who.

2. The offer, made concrete

Describe the lead magnet as a thing with a shape: pages, minutes, steps. Vague value (“insights”) does not earn an email address.

3. Proof someone else trusted you

Logos, numbers or quotes. One real sentence from a customer outperforms three generic ones. Never invent them.

4. The form, short and visible

Ask only for what your follow-up needs. Explain what happens after submit. Make the button describe the outcome, not the action.

5. Objections answered

An FAQ handles the last doubts (price, privacy, time, fit) without a sales call, and doubles as structured data for search engines.

6. A final, repeated call to action

Visitors who scrolled to the end are the most convinced. Give them the same action again, not a different one.

The flow

From click to conversation in four steps

Step 1: Attract with one message

Every ad, post or email that points here says the same thing as the headline. Message match is the cheapest conversion lift available.

Step 2: Remove friction

No menu, no footer full of links, a page that loads in under two seconds on mobile. This page keeps only a logo and one button in the header.

Step 3: Capture the minimum

Two fields and a consent box. Progressive profiling can ask for more later, once the lead has a reason to answer.

Step 4: Deliver instantly, follow up deliberately

Send the asset in the confirmation email, then a short sequence that earns the reply. The landing page’s job ended at submit; the sequence’s job starts there.

FAQ

Questions people ask before they fill in a form

Answering them here saves a sales call and gives search engines a rich result.

What is a lead generation landing page?

A single page with one goal: exchange something valuable (a guide, a demo, a quote, a trial) for a visitor’s contact details. Unlike a homepage it has no competing navigation and one call to action repeated throughout.

How many form fields should a lead form have?

As few as your follow-up needs. Name and email are enough for a content download; add company or budget only when sales will actually use them. Every extra required field lowers completion.

Where should the form go: above the fold or at the end?

Both. Put a short form or a button that jumps to it in the hero for visitors who arrive convinced, and repeat the form after the proof section for those who need the full story first.

What is a good conversion rate for a lead landing page?

It depends on traffic source and offer. Cold paid traffic often converts in the low single digits; warm email or retargeting traffic can reach 10–20 %. Compare against your own baseline and test one change at a time.

How do I make the form actually send submissions with AstroWind?

The Form component ships without a backend on purpose. Add method="POST" and an action (Netlify Forms, Formspree, Web3Forms or your own endpoint) in src/components/ui/Form.astro. See .agents/skills/configure-contact-form.md in the repository.

Does this page work for GDPR / privacy consent?

The form includes a consent checkbox linked to the privacy policy. Keep the label explicit about what you will send and how often, and store consent with the lead.
